Phase 1 — external reference, session of 2026-11-19 (evening)
==============================================================
Arm: arm1 (so100_cell), passive reads, torque ON only while a pose was held, arming and disarming
done by tools/joint_reference.py. Arm resting/raised on the bench; no support under the forearm.

Instrument: handheld digital inclinometer, read to 0.1 deg by the operator and dictated.
Mounting: on a flat face of the link, axis along the link; the instrument's measuring plane was
re-mounted between the first series and the second (see "invalidated series").

Protocol per joint: hold a pose, read /read_raw and the instrument at the same pose, then step the
joint and repeat. The SDK value and the encoder tick are recorded with each reading; for the elbow
the shoulder value is recorded too, because the instrument on the forearm measures absolute
inclination, i.e. shoulder + elbow.

Results (least squares, external = slope * SDK + offset)
-------------------------------------------------------
shoulder_lift   n=3   slope 0.990   span 15.2 deg   offset 87.4 deg
                segments 0.835 (reversal) / 1.113 (same direction)
elbow_flex      n=3   slope 0.976   span  7.5 deg   offset -6.7 deg   (fit on shoulder+elbow)
                segments 0.914 / 0.972

Reading: the SDK unit is a degree, to within the resolution of this session. Over a 15 deg span and
0.1 deg readings the slope uncertainty is about +-0.02, so 0.990 is consistent with 1.000 and also
with a real 1 % scale error; tightening it needs a wider span (~30 deg) or repeated readings per pose.

Two other findings, both recorded because they are what a controller has to live with:

- Backlash / lag on reversal, about 1 deg: for shoulder_lift the reversal segment loses 1.1 deg and
  the next same-direction segment gives it back (0.835 then 1.113, summing to 0.986 over the pair).
  The elbow shows the same shape more weakly (0.914 then 0.972).
- The joint does not reach the commanded angle under this load: elbow commanded -10.0 deg arrived at
  -4.84 (5.2 short) and +10.0 arrived at +10.99; shoulder commanded -10.0 arrived at -6.94 (3.1 short)
  and +6.0 arrived at +6.68. Commanded, achieved and physical are three different numbers, which is
  why the owner re-reads the encoders and never treats a write as a pose.

Invalidated series
------------------
The first shoulder_lift series (files ref_shoulder_lift_arm1_oldmount.csv) was taken before the
instrument was re-mounted: the same pose read 78.0 deg then and 75.9 deg afterwards. A change of
mounting changes the offset but not the slope, so that series is kept as history and not used in any
fit. It is the reason every row carries the instrument name.

Not measured in this session

- repeatability: one reading per pose; no repeated readings, so the reading noise is not separated

wrist_roll — 2026-11-19, arm 1, printed 80 mm disk
--------------------------------------------------
R0 -85.76 (tick 1072) -> 9.0 deg; +20 -> R1 -65.98 (1297) -> 29.0; +20 -> R2 -46.20 (1522) -> 51.0;
then -39.78 back to R3 -85.85 (1071) -> 9.0, the same reading as R0: no hysteresis at the endpoints.

Segment slopes: 1.011 (first 20 deg), 1.112 (second 20 deg), total 1.062 over 42 deg of disk.
Ticks per degree 11.38 against 4095/360 = 11.375, so the SDK value is the encoder itself and the
discrepancy is not in the bridge.

Interpretation: the instrument, not the joint, is what failed here.
Error against the SDK: +0.2 deg at 20, +2.4 at 40, +8.2 at 60, **+27.9 at 120**. Segment slopes 1.011,
1.112, 1.138, 1.234. A round trip of 240 deg brings the disk back to exactly 9.0 at -85.50 (tick 1075
against 1072 at the start), so nothing slipped and the joint returns to where it was.

That combination rules things out rather than leaving them open:
- scale error: a 6 % scale would give ~7 deg at 120, not 28;
- eccentric card: bounded by 2e/r (about 1.4 deg for 1 mm of offset) and back to zero at 180;
- slip of the card or of the coupling: excluded by the exact return.
What is left is the **mounting plane**: a card whose face is not perpendicular to the roll axis makes the
apparent angle non-uniform, and the distortion grows with the turn. Reversible, superlinear, no slip.

So: this series is kept as a record of a bad instrument. Only the first 40 deg (slope 1.01-1.11) is
usable to say the sense and the order of magnitude agree; wrist_roll is NOT signed off.

Next attempt, in order of preference: a line drawn with a permanent marker directly on the rotating
collar, read against a fixed mark on the forearm (no card, no plane, nothing to slip); or the same disk
mounted on a face turned square to the axis and centred on it. Ticks per degree 11.38 against
4095/360 = 11.375 confirms the bridge copies the encoder faithfully, so the next series only needs to
test the mechanics.

shoulder_pan — 2026-11-19, arm 1, printed 190 mm sheet on the table
--------------------------------------------------------------------
Pointer taped to the rotating link, tip sweeping on the paper; sheet centred on the pan axis, 0 deg at
the front of the bench. Points: 0, 32, 63, 94, 123 deg of sheet against 0, 29.36, 58.46, 88.17, 116.04
of SDK, then back to 0.

Fit over 0..116: external = **1.0589 . SDK + 0.56 deg**, max residual 0.56 deg (pure scale without the
offset: 1.0652, rms 0.53). Segment ratios fall from 1.090 to 1.041, so the first segment is the weakest
reading, not a curvature. Ticks per degree 11.38 again, consistent with 4095/360.

**Pan is about 6 % faster than its own degrees.** A commanded 90 deg lands near 95 physical deg. That is
the largest real discrepancy of the three joints measured so far (shoulder_lift 0.990, elbow_flex 0.976)
and it is in the opposite direction.

Reversibility: the return lands on tick 2101, the same tick it left from, and the pointer reads 2 deg
where it read 0 — so backlash is at most ~2 deg, and the encoder lost no steps over 232 deg of travel.

Travel: asking 126 deg stalls at 116.00 (two sub-steps of 10 commanded, no movement, the stall guard
stopped the pose). So the useful pan travel is roughly +-116, not the +-170 the driver's clamp suggests;
the clamp is a fuse, not the travel range.

wrist_flex — 2026-11-19, arm 1, hand-held inclinometer
------------------------------------------------------
W1 56.53 (tick 2623) 57.0; W2 32.18 (2346) 36.9; W3 8.70 (2079) 13.8; back to W4 56.53 (2623) 60.5.
shoulder_lift and elbow_flex recorded at each pose because the instrument measures absolute inclination
and those two drift about 1.5 deg under load; the corrected segments come out 0.92 and 1.01, total 0.96.

The decisive line is W4: **the same SDK value and the same tick 2623 as W1, and the instrument reads 60.5
where it read 57.0.** The joint did not move; the inclinometer did, because it is placed by hand on a small
link. That 3.5 deg is the instrument's placement error, and it is three times larger than the effects this
series was meant to resolve — which is why the 0.92/1.01 split is scatter and not a cos(23 deg) projection.

Verdict: wrist_flex is **not signed off**, but nothing suggests a bad joint either: the encoder returns to
the identical tick, so the mechanics repeat. What is missing is an instrument that stays put.

Fix for the next attempt: clamp or tape the inclinometer to the link (the earlier shoulder series was
usable precisely because the instrument rested on a flat face), then three poses over 40-50 deg plus a
return, and read W1 vs W4 as the check that the instrument did not move.

wrist_flex, second series — instrument taped to the link
--------------------------------------------------------
A1 69.54 (tick 2771) 46.1; A2 46.24 (2506) 24.2; A3 back to 69.45 (2770) 46.1. shoulder_lift and
elbow_flex were flat to 0.09 deg across the sweep, so no drift correction is needed here.

**Slope 0.937** over 23.3 deg, and the return to A3 reproduces A1 to 0.0 deg with the tick within 1:
unlike the hand-held series (B, whose return was 3.5 deg off), this instrument did not move.

Why 0.937 is taken as the joint and not as a mounting artefact: a tilt of the instrument out of the plane
of rotation multiplies every reading by cos(phi) and is constant, so it cannot be told apart from a scale
error in a single mounting — but two *different* mountings were used. Hand-held gave 0.96 over 48 deg,
taped gave 0.937 over 23 deg; two different tilts landing within 0.03 of each other is a coincidence
much less likely than a joint that is about 5 % short.

Verdict: wrist_flex = **0.94 +- 0.02**, signed off with that caveat written down (single mounting would
not have been enough). What is still missing: a longer arc, a repeat, and a loaded point.

wrist_roll, third attempt — read along the axis
-----------------------------------------------
Same two permanent-marker lines as the second attempt; only the viewing direction changed: this time the
operator looked **along the forearm axis** (the cylinder projects as a circle, so the angle between the
marks is undistorted). R0 -85.67 (tick 1073) 7.0; +30 steps to 32.0, 58.0, 85.0; back to -85.23 (1078) 7.0.

Measured deltas 25.0 / 51.0 / 78.0 against SDK 29.84 / 59.69 / 88.88: least squares gives
**external = 0.877 . SDK - 0.61 deg**, residual at most 0.73 deg, segment slopes 0.838 / 0.854 / 0.878.
(The hand fit between the two ends gives 0.898; the four-point least squares is the one quoted.) Return reproduces the zero exactly, and the
tick is 5 apart (about 0.4 deg), so nothing slipped and no steps were lost.

Contrast with the first two attempts: the card read obliquely gave 1.09, then 1.14, then 1.24 — the error
growing with the turn — and the hand-read collar without a fixed viewpoint gave 1.24 as well. Reading along
the axis gives three slopes that agree to 0.04. **The viewpoint was the instrument error, and the joint is
about 12 % short**: a commanded 90 deg turns the wrist about 79.

Caveat kept on the record: one mounting, one direction of travel for the sweep, 1 deg readings. The number
is signed at 0.88 +- 0.03, which is enough to put it in the margin and not enough to call it exact.
